☕ Stocks-to-use & price

World stocks-to-use sets the flat price level; ICE certified stocks-to-use sets the front of the curve (KC1 − KC2) and how violently the nearby trades. Trading flat price starts with forecasting stocks-to-use, then tracking the next event that will move it.

4 · Certified stocks relative to world stocks

Exchange share = (certified / use) ÷ (world STU): the part of the world's stocks that sits in ICE warehouses and can be delivered. When it collapses while world STU is stable, the coffee exists but not where the futures can reach it → the front spread and KC1 trade above what the balance sheet alone justifies.

5 · The two relationships

6 · Brazil quality spreads

Quality spreads decide whether Brazilian coffee is worth certifying. When fine/good cup trade at a premium to NY, it goes to the physical market, not to ICE; when fine cup − low grade blows out, deliverable-quality coffee is scarce relative to the crop.

7 · Reading — what happens to price when certified STU is higher or lower than world STU?

Two layers, two prices

  • World STU → flat price level (slow, seasonal). Driven by Brazil production (arabica on/off year, frost/drought), Vietnam robusta and demand growth. 2010/11–2020/21 STU 18–29% ⇒ KC mostly 100–200 c (2011 spike aside); 2022/23–2026/27 STU 13–16% ⇒ KC roughly 150–420 c.
  • Certified STU → front spread & nearby premium (fast, daily). Certified stocks are the only stocks the futures can deliver. Their level against world use sets KC1 − KC2.

Certified STU low relative to world STU (today)

  • Front spread goes to inverse (KC1 > KC2): shorts must buy back or deliver coffee that is not in the warehouses → squeezes into each first notice day, high volatility.
  • KC1 trades above what world STU implies; the flat price is "held up by the front". The curve is backwardated, so the deferreds price in the rebuild.
  • Inverse itself keeps stocks out of the exchange: with no carry, nobody is paid to store and certify.

Certified STU high relative to world STU (2016–2021)

  • Full carry (KC1 − KC2 ≈ −cost of storage), front capped by deliverable supply; KC1 follows world STU only, with low volatility.
  • The exchange acts as a buffer — a supply shock first drains certified stocks before the spread can invert (e.g. after the July 2021 frost the spread stayed near carry while certs were still above 1% of use, and only inverted in 2022 as they fell toward 0.5%).

Brazil quality spreads↓ when diffs > NYIf fine / good cup sell at or above NY in the physical market, exporters sell there instead of grading for ICE. Certification only pays when the physical diff is below NY minus grading, freight and warehouse costs.
Harvest quality (expected vs realised)↓ if crop is low-gradeA big crop that is mostly rio / low grade widens FC − LG but adds little deliverable coffee. Watch the share of fine cup in the 2026/27 harvest and grading pass rates.
Backwardation↓ self-reinforcingAn inverted curve pays you to sell physical now rather than store in an exchange warehouse.
Strong demand / roaster pull↓Roasters take delivery and withdraw bags when origin is tight; certificates are the supply of last resort.
Trade policy / logistics↓US tariff on Brazil (Aug 2025, lifted for coffee Nov 2025) kept Brazilian coffee out of US ports; freight and port delays slow arrivals to grading.
Ageing / decertification↓Old lots are penalised then lose their certificate — the stock decays unless it is replaced.
Farmer selling, BRL↑ when farmers sellStrong BRL + high prices = farmers hold; weak BRL / cash needs = flows to exporters and potentially to grading.
Return to carry↑Once KC1 − KC2 drops under the cost of carry, storage pays again and grading flows accelerate (watch the spread vs bags in grading).

Now (Oct 2026) — the next event to track

  • ICE certified arabica hit a 27-year low (217,646 bags on 15 Sep 2026) then rebounded to ~260k bags — the first new-crop Brazilian lots reaching grading. KC1 − KC2 has dropped from the +30 c area back to single digits.
  • The test is whether the 2026/27 Brazil harvest realisation (volume and fine-cup share) keeps feeding grading. If quality spreads compress (FC − LG back under ~50 c and fine cup back to a discount to NY), certs rebuild → spread back toward carry → KC1 loses its front premium and converges down to what world STU (~14–15%) implies.
  • If the harvest disappoints on quality, or physical diffs stay above NY, certs stay near 0.15–0.25% of use → spread re-inverts into the Dec/Mar deliveries and KC1 stays bid even with a looser world balance.
